# Demo Narrative Quality

> The demo tells a story. Pre-event feels like planning stress — things are incomplete, follow-ups
> needed, gaps to fill. During-event feels like live ops — everything moving, drivers en route,
> guests in panels, next pickup in 20 minutes. Post-event feels like wrap-up satisfaction — archived,
> analytics visible, ready for next year. The canvas walkthrough is the "aha" moment.

---

## Overview

A technically correct demo with bad narrative is just a spreadsheet with better CSS. The demo must
make convention staff feel "this is what my job looks like" at each phase. The data, the dashboard
numbers, the alerts, the completion percentages — all must tell a coherent story that builds
credibility with non-technical users.

---

## Verification Steps

### 1. Pre-Event Narrative (2 Weeks Before)

Check that the pre-event state creates productive tension:

- [ ] Dashboard shows incomplete work — NOT everything green. Target: ~45% prep complete.
- [ ] At least 2-3 guests still in early states (draft/invited) requiring follow-up
- [ ] At least 3 overdue prep items creating urgency
- [ ] At least 1 staffing gap (guest without liaison or interpreter)
- [ ] Schedule has some empty slots and at least 1 venue conflict
- [ ] Transport bookings mostly in "requested" state — not yet confirmed
- [ ] The feeling: "We have work to do, but we can see what needs doing"

### 2. During-Event Narrative (Saturday Morning)

Check that the during-event state creates operational energy:

- [ ] Most guests in arrived/attending states
- [ ] At least 2 guests currently in events (panels, autographs)
- [ ] At least 1 active transport run (driver waiting or en route)
- [ ] At least 1 time-sensitive item (next pickup in N minutes)
- [ ] Prep is 85-90% complete — almost there but not perfect
- [ ] Schedule shows "now" indicator on current events
- [ ] The feeling: "Things are happening right now, I know what's going on"

### 3. Post-Event Narrative (After Convention)

Check that the post-event state creates satisfaction and forward-looking energy:

- [ ] All guests in departed status
- [ ] Prep 95%+ complete (some things always get skipped)
- [ ] Convention marked as archived
- [ ] Analytics visible: return rates, department breakdown, year-over-year
- [ ] Registry shows multi-year guest history
- [ ] The feeling: "We did it, and next year starts with this knowledge"

### 4. Canvas Walkthrough Narrative

Check that the canvas view creates understanding:

- [ ] System graph shows clear concept relationships (Guest → Pairing → Staff, Event → Venue)
- [ ] Workflow overlay shows the "Guest Confirmed" cascade clearly
- [ ] Data flow overlay shows the fan-out pattern (guest data → GR, PR, Transport)
- [ ] Department boundary is visible and meaningful
- [ ] A non-technical person can explain what they see in 1-2 sentences
- [ ] The feeling: "Oh, THAT'S how it all connects"

### 5. Emotional Progression

The 4 tabs should feel like a journey:
- Pre-event: anticipation + productive stress → "We have a plan"
- During-event: energy + confidence → "We're on top of this"
- Post-event: satisfaction + intelligence → "We know what worked"
- Canvas: revelation + trust → "This system understands our operations"

---

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Non-technical reviewer can describe what each view shows without prompting
- [ ] Dashboard numbers tell a story (not random — progress is visible between states)
- [ ] Alerts and warnings create appropriate urgency without panic
- [ ] The canvas walkthrough produces an "aha" reaction
- [ ] Data feels realistic to someone who has run a convention
